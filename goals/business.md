# Learn business domain

**Slug:** `business` (matches `work/business/`)

**One sentence:**
Become an SWE who understands the **business domain** of the software I build — who uses it, what job it does in their work, and the rules and language that matter — so I design and explain systems as domain software, not only as code from a spec.

**Why this, now:**
I work in Japanese outsourcing: I build from the client’s requirements. I am strong at backend, architecture, and databases, but I often stay coding-only — the spec stands in for the domain. That is not enough for mid-level overseas, for teaching, or for work where the software has to be right for the business. I need the domain of a **real system I touch**, in my own words. Generic business-model reading is not this goal.

**What “done” looks like:**
I can explain, in my own words, the domain of at least one real system I work on: the users or client roles, what the software is for in their business, the important terms, and 2–3 rules or constraints that would make a design wrong if I ignored them. I have notes / essay / practice files under `work/business/` from that system — not from a canvas or MBA book. I have used that language when designing, reviewing, or teaching, not only in reading notes.

**What I will not chase in the name of this goal:**
Generic business books as a substitute for a domain I touch (*The Personal MBA*, Business Model Canvas, LTV/CAC grind with no system of mine). Founder or MBA identity. An industry I do not work in. Newsletters. Letting this steal every 9–5 practice slot from DevOps without naming which goal that day’s work serves. Counting reading alone as done.
