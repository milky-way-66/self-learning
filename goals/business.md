# Learn business

**Slug:** `business` (matches `work/business/`)

**One sentence:**
Become an SWE who can connect what we build to money, cost, and the metric a bet is meant to move — and use that at work so I can join (and contribute in) a product company, not only ship code.

**Why this, now:**
I am stuck in a coding-heavy shape of the job. Software without a clear business model is hard for me to reason about, and that blocks moving into a product company where the work is creating value, not only features. I need to talk and think about money, customers, and unit economics well enough to apply what I learn in real work conversations — roadmap, cost, “why this bet” — not only in reading notes. Path: **workplace influence + operator literacy first (heavy on workplace)**; builder/founder later when I am ready. Literacy alone is a step; apply-at-work is the point.

**What “done” looks like:**
I can explain, in my own words, a foundation of business language (value, cost, customer, constraint, strategy) from at least one core book (*The Personal MBA* or equivalent) and use it to judge a real decision at work. I can define and teach LTV, CAC, churn (and Rule of 40 when SaaS-relevant), and apply them to a product I know (employer product or a written case). I have done at least two fieldwork practices with files under `work/business/` (e.g. PM roadmap walkthrough — “what metric this quarter?”; sales/marketing “why customers say no”; cloud/cost vs value for something I touch). I have used this in real work talk — not only notes. Builder/founder (path C) is out of scope for this “done.”

**What I will not chase in the name of this goal:**
Course certificates and Wharton-as-badge. Newsletter / Stratechery lifestyle with no notes that change a decision. Founder cosplay, monetization fantasy, or a product lab before I am ready for path C. Generic “leadership MBA” disconnected from work I touch. Letting business steal every 9–5 practice slot from DevOps without naming which goal that day’s work serves. Counting reading alone as done.
